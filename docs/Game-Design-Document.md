## **Always A (Trivial) Triple Threat** 

## **Game Design Document & Rules Reference** 

## **1. Core Concept** 

**Always A (Trivial) Triple Threat** is a trivia game connecting **Video Games** , **Animation** (including comics, manga, and graphic novels), and **Pro Wrestling** . 

The game eschews random trivia in favour of "The Chain"—cleverly linked questions that highlight the strange and fascinating interconnections between these three domains. 

## **2. The Three Pillars (Category Definitions)** 

To prevent player arguments, these are the official "House Rules" regarding what fits into each category. 

## 📺 **ANIMATION** 

The entire spectrum of **Drawn & Illustrated Visual Storytelling** . This category is **NOT** limited to **Definition:** moving pictures. If the primary medium is hand-drawn, CGI, or illustrated art, it counts. 

- **Included:** 

   - **Traditional Animation:** Cartoons, Anime, CGI films, Stop-Motion. 

   - **Sequential Art:** Comic Books, Manga, Graphic Novels, Webcomics. 

   - **Illustrated Literature:** Children’s Picture Books (e.g., _Mr. Men_ , _Berenstain Bears_ ). 

   - **Newspaper Strips:** (e.g., _Peanuts_ , _Calvin & Hobbes_ ). 

## 🎮 **VIDEO GAMES** 

The history, culture, and content of **interactive digital entertainment Definition:** . If software requires 

player input to manipulate images on a display to achieve a goal, it is a Video Game. 

- **Included:** 

   - Arcade, Console, PC, Mobile. 

   - **All Platforms:** 

   - **All Genres:** FPS, RPG, Visual Novels, Puzzle Games (e.g., _Wordle_ ). 

   - **Esports:** Competitive gaming history and teams. 

   - **Edutainment:** Classic educational titles ( _The Oregon Trail_ ). 

## **🤼 PRO WRESTLING** 

**Professional Wrestling / Sports Entertainment** . This covers the scripted performance art form, **Definition:** including on-screen storylines ("Kayfabe") and real-life backstage history. 

- **Included:** 

   - **All Promotions:** WWE/WWF, WCW, ECW, AEW, TNA, etc. 

   - **Global:** Puroresu (Japan), Lucha Libre (Mexico), British Wrestling. 

   - **The Indies:** ROH, PWG, independent performers. 

   - **Reality:** Promoter history, backstage news, and wrestler biographies. 

## **3. Gameplay Structure** 

## **Basic Round Structure** 

Each round consists of **three connected questions** , one from each domain. The questions are designed so the answer to one leads into the context of the next. 

## **Example Round (The "Tekken" Chain):** 

1. **(Video Games):** "Which fighting game series features a character named King who wears a jaguar mask 

and uses wrestling moves?" 

   - _Answer:_ **Tekken** 

2. **(Animation):** "In what animated series did Tekken characters appear alongside Street Fighter characters?" 

   - _Answer:_ **Street Fighter X Tekken: The Animation** 

3. **(Pro Wrestling):** "Which WWE Hall of Famer was known as 'The Excellence of Execution' and was referenced by a character in Street Fighter?" 

   - _Answer:_ **Bret "The Hitman" Hart** _(Referenced by T. Hawk's "Hart Attack" move)_ 

## **Connection Types** 

Questions are linked through these primary themes: 

- **Alter Egos:** Characters with dual identities or transformations. 

- **Locations:** Shared or similar settings across media. 

- **Factions/Groups:** Teams, stables, organisations. 

- **Objects:** Iconic items, weapons, or artefacts. 

- **Rivalries:** Notable feuds and confrontations. 

- **Shared Terminology:** Industry terms with parallel meanings (e.g., "Face/Heel" in wrestling vs. "Hero/Villain" in comics). 

## **4. Gameplay Rules: How to Play** 

The game is played in sets (Decks) of 10–20 questions. Each Deck has an overarching **Theme** (e.g., "90s **Links** between questions may diverge from that Nostalgia," "Spooky Season," "Robots"), but the specific theme to maintain the chain. 

## **The Setup** 

1. **Teams:** Players divide into Teams (or play as individuals). 

2. **The Deck:** A pre-written sequence of linked questions. 

3. **The "Boss Battle" (List Questions):** 

   - _Placement:_ List questions work best as **Checkpoints** (e.g., Question 5, 10, 15) or the **Finale** . 

   - _Function:_ They break the rhythm and serve as a "skill check" for the active team. 

## **The Turn Loop** 

1. **The Question:** The Host directs the question to the **Active Team** . 

2. **The Answer Phase:** 

   - **Standard Questions:** 

      - **Correct:** The Active Team gains **1 Point** . 

      - **Incorrect:** The question **passes** to the next team in line for a steal. 

      - **The Steal: 1 Point** . If incorrect, it The next team may attempt to answer. If correct, they gain 

      - passes again. 

      - **Dead End:** If no team answers correctly, the Host reveals the answer (No points awarded). 

   - **List Questions (All-or-Nothing Rule):** 

      - To win the point, the team must provide **ALL** requested items in the list. (e.g., naming only 3 out of 4 requested items counts as **Incorrect** ). 

      - **Default Mode (Incomplete Steal):** If the Active Team fails, the next team in line only has to provide the _remaining_ missing items to steal the point. 

      - **Hard Mode (Full Re-run):** The stealing team must recite the _entire_ list from scratch to earn the point. 

      - _Note:_ The Host must declare which Mode is active before the deck begins. 

3. **The Link Phase:** 

   - Once the answer is established, the Host reveals the **Link** to the next question. 

   - _Note:_ The Link may be subtle ("Speaking of that year...") or explicit ("That actor also voiced..."). 

4. **Next Turn:** The next question begins. 

   - **Alternating Initiative:** The "Active Team" status rotates for every new question, regardless of who answered the previous one correctly (e.g., Team A gets Q1, Team B gets Q2, Team A gets Q3). 

## **Winning** 

- The team with the highest score at the end of the Deck wins. 

- **Sudden Death Tie-Breaker:** In the event of a tie, the Host asks a numerical approximation question (e.g., "How many days did [Wrestler] hold the belt?"). The closest guess wins.

## **Category Violations** 

- 🟥 **RED CARD 1: THE "KAYFABE" VIOLATION** 

   - _Argument:_ "But wrestling is fake!" 

   - _Ruling:_ Within this game, wrestling storylines are treated as factual history. 

- 🟥 **RED CARD 2: THE "MMA" VIOLATION** 

   - _Argument:_ "Does UFC count?" 

   - _Ruling:_ Boxing and MMA are Combat Sports, not Pro Wrestling. Unless it happened in a wrestling ring 

      - (e.g., Floyd Mayweather vs. Big Show), it is disqualified. 

- 🟥 **RED CARD 3: THE "REMAKE" VIOLATION** 

   - _Argument:_ "But in the Live-Action movie..." 

   - _Ruling:_ Marketing defines the medium. A live-action remake does not change the answer to a 

   - question about the original animated film.

## **Canon Violations** 

- 🟥 **RED CARD 8: THE "BOOTLEG" VIOLATION** 

   - _Argument:_ "But that happened in the _Kaizo Mario_ mod!" 

   - _Ruling:_ **OFFICIAL RELEASES ONLY.** Fan mods, ROM hacks, and fan-fiction are invalid unless the

- 🟥 **RED CARD 9: THE "TOYBOX" VIOLATION** 

   - _Argument:_ "His action figure came with a laser gun!" 

   - _Ruling:_ **SCREEN TRUMPS PLASTIC.** Toy accessories do not override the source material (Game/Show/Match).

## **Gameplay & Mechanics Violations** 

- 🟥 **RED CARD 11: THE "MOTION" VIOLATION** 

   - _Argument:_ "That's not animation, it's a book/comic!" 

   - _Ruling:_ **DENIED.** 

   - _Verdict:_ If it is drawn, painted, or illustrated, IT COUNTS as Visual Storytelling. 

- 🟥 **RED CARD 12: THE "CASUAL" VIOLATION** 

   - _Argument:_ "Candy Crush isn't a REAL video game!" 

   - _Ruling:_ **DENIED.** 

   - _Verdict:_ If it is electronic, interactive, and has a win/loss state or narrative, IT IS A VIDEO GAME. (e.g., _Angry Birds_ is as valid as _Elden Ring_ .) 

- 🟥 **RED CARD 13: THE "RETCON" VIOLATION** 

   - _Argument:_ "Disney wiped that from canon!" or "That was changed in the 2015 reboot!" 

   - _Ruling:_ **ICONIC STATUS WINS.** 

   - _Verdict:_ Unless the question specifies "Current Continuity," the most famous/original version of the 

   - fact is accepted. 

- 🟥 **RED CARD 14: THE "IMPORT" VIOLATION** 

   - _Argument:_ "It's Biohazard, not Resident Evil!" or "It's technically the Famicom, not NES!" 

   - _Ruling:_ **ACCEPTED (BUT DON'T BE SMUG).** 

   - _Verdict:_ Both the original Japanese title and the localised title are correct. No bonus points for showing off. 

- 🟥 **RED CARD 15: THE "SEMANTICS" VIOLATION** 

   - _Argument:_ "Technically, Link is a Hylian, not a human" or "Frankenstein is the Doctor, not the Monster." 

   - _Ruling:_ **INTENT OVER SYNTAX.** 

   - _Verdict:_ Colloquial answers are accepted unless the question is a specific "Trick Question" designed 

   - to test the misconception. 

- 🟥 **RED CARD 16: THE "SPEEDRUN" VIOLATION** 

   - _Argument:_ "Actually, you don't need the Blue Key if you use the wall-clip glitch." 

   - _Ruling:_ **INTENDED PLAY ONLY.** 

   - _Verdict:_ Answers must reflect the standard, intended narrative or gameplay experience, not code- 

   - breaking, glitches, or sequence breaks.

## **Question Writer's Guide: Balancing The Triple Threat** 

This guide helps you categorise questions to ensure a fair mix of difficulty. A good trivia night usually follows a **20/40/30/10** split (20% Easy, 40% Medium, 30% Hard, 10% Expert). 

## **1. The Difficulty Scale** 

## 🟢 **Level 1: CASUAL (The "Pop Culture" Tier)** 

**Target:** Solvable by someone who has seen the movie once, played the game casually, or watches wrestling only during WrestleMania. 

- **Focus:** Main characters, subtitles, famous catchphrases, basic premises. 

- **The "Bar Test":** If you shouted this question in a crowded bar, would at least 3 people know it? 

## **Examples:** 

- _(Video Games)_ : "What colour are Mario's suspenders?" (Blue) 

- _(Animation)_ : "Who lives in a pineapple under the sea?" (SpongeBob SquarePants) 

- _(Wrestling)_ : "Which wrestler's catchphrase is 'You Can't See Me'?" (John Cena) 

## 🟡 **Level 2: FAN (The "Hobbyist" Tier)** 

**Target:** Solvable by someone who enjoys the genre and pays attention to details. 

- **Focus:** Side characters, plot specifics, approximate release years, voice actors, finishing moves. 

- **The "Bar Test":** You need to find the table wearing comic book t-shirts to get this answered. 

## **Examples:** 

- _(Video Games)_ : "What is the name of the ghost that acts as the referee in _Super Mario Kart_ ?" (Lakitu)

## **4. Preventing "Category Creep" (Safety Checks)** 

To ensure "The Chain" remains unbroken, all questions must pass the following **Safety Checks** before being included in a deck. 

## **CHECK 1: The "Medium Specification" Rule** 

Because franchises overlap, you **MUST** specify which version you are asking about. 

- ❌ _"Who is the Gym Leader of Pewter City?"_ (Ambiguous—different in Game vs. Anime). 

- ❌ _"In the_ _**original Pokémon Red & Blue video games** , who is the Gym Leader of Pewter City?"_ 

## **CHECK 2: The "Primary Source" Test** 

We only accept answers based on what happened **On Screen, In Game, or In the Ring** . 

- ❌ _"What is the Decepticon toaster's name?"_ (Obscure toy bio). 

- ❌ _"In the_ _**1980s G1 Cartoon** , which Decepticon was ejected from Astrotrain?"_ (Screen canon). 

**CHECK 3: The "Cross-Over" Containment** 

Classify questions by the **media** , not the IP. 

- _The Last of Us_ (HBO Show) = **TV/General Knowledge** (INVALID). 

- _The Last of Us_ (PS5 Game) = **Video Game** (VALID). 

- _Troy Baker_ (Voice Actor) = **Video Game** (VALID). 

## **CHECK 4: The "Time-Lock" Protocol** 

Lore changes. Champions lose belts. Games get patched. **Time-Lock** your facts. 

- ❌ _"Who is the WWE Champion?"_ 

- ❌ _"Who defeated Roman Reigns at_ _**WrestleMania 40** ?"_ 

- ❌ _"Does McCree’s flashbang stun?"_ 

- ❌ _"In the_ _**launch version of Overwatch 1** , did the flashbang stun?"_ 

## **CHECK 5: The "Subjectivity" Ban** 

Never ask for opinions ("Best," "Coolest," "Most Popular") unless tied to a metric. 

- ❌ _"What is the best Zelda game?"_ 

- ❌ _"Which Zelda game has the_ _**highest Metacritic score** ?"_ 

## **CHECK 6: The "List Question" Protocol (Creation Guide)** 

List questions are high-risk. Follow these rules to make them playable. 

- **Rule A: The "Closed Loop" (Finite Answers):** Never ask for a list drawn from an infinite pool. 

   - ❌ _"Name three Sonic games."_ (Too easy, boring, thousands of answers). 

- ❌ _"Name the_ _**three Sonic games released on the Sega Dreamcast** ."_ (Finite, specific, challenging). 

- ● **Rule B: The "Goldilocks" Number:** The ideal list size is **3 to 5 items** . 

   - _Less than 3:_ Just ask a normal question. 

   - _More than 5:_ Slows down the game too much. 

- **Rule C: The "All-or-Nothing" Tag:** Explicitly state if the player needs _all_ of them or just _some_ of them. 

   - _"Name_ _**any two** members of Evolution."_ 

   - _"Name_ _**all four** members of the Beatles."_ 

## **CHECK 7: The "Specifics" Trap** 

Avoid phrasing that implies a single answer when multiple exist. 

- **Trap:** "Name the Nintendo Adventure Book." (Implies only one exists). 

- **Fix:** "Name Nintendo Adventure Books." **five of the twelve** 

- **Trap:** "Who played Mario in the movie?" (Implies only Bob Hoskins). 

- **Fix:** "Name the **two actors** who played the Mario Bros in the 1993 film." 

## **CHECK 8: The "Bridge or Bench" Rule (Link Integrity)** 

A perfect question with a weak link is a failed question. If the connection requires a paragraph of explanation, it is broken. 

- **Option A (BRIDGE IT):** Insert an intermediate question to smooth the transition. 

- **Option B (BENCH IT):** Remove the question and save it for a future deck where it fits naturally. 

- **Option C (KILL IT):** If it cannot be bridged or banked, delete it. Never force a weak link just to keep a "good question." 

## **CHECK 9: The "Deck Balance" Rule** 

Unless the Deck is explicitly themed (e.g., "The Anime Special"), Writers must aim for equilibrium. 

- **The 33% Standard:** A standard 15-question deck should ideally have ~5 questions from each pillar. 

- **The "Dominance" Limit:** No single category should exceed 50% of the deck (unless it is a Theme Deck). 

- **The "Link" Flow:** Try not to link Game -> Game -> Game. Weave between categories to keep all players engaged.

## **7. The Theme Encyclopedia** 

To ensure variety in "The Chain," questions should be grouped into specific themes. Themes help structure the links and provide flavour to each Round. 

## **Tier 1: The Core Themes (Standard Play)** 

These themes rely on clear, factual links suitable for all difficulty levels. 

- **Certain Years & Decades:** Context-based links (e.g., "1998" links to _Ocarina of Time_ ). _The Attitude Era_ 

## ● **Region-Based:** 

   - _Only In America:_ Hollywood, WWE, Western RPGs. 

   - _U WOT M8? (The British Invasion):_ British Wrestling (World of Sport/NXT UK), Rare/Rockstar North, Aardman/Gorillaz. 

   - _Nani? (Japan):_ Puroresu, Nintendo/Sony, Anime/Manga. 

- **Known By Another Name:** Links based on aliases, rebrands, or alterations (e.g., _Biohazard_ vs. _Resident Evil_ ; Mean Mark Callous vs. The Undertaker). 

- **Colours & Music:** Links based on visual motifs or audio (e.g., Fictional Bands, Entrance Themes, Rhythm Games). 

- **Double Cross(overs):** The "Meta-Theme." Links based on characters appearing in other franchises (e.g., _Kingdom Hearts_ , _Space Jam_ , _Smash Bros_ ). 

## **Tier 2: High Potential (Strong Narrative Links)** 

- **Masked Marvels:** Luchadores, Masked Vigilantes, Helmeted Protagonists. 

- **Family Business:** Real-life siblings (The Harts), fictional families (The Marios), or clans. 

- **Royalty & Rulers:** "King" gimmicks, Princesses, Emperors. 

- **Weapons of Choice:** Signature items (Sledgehammers, Buster Swords, Batarangs). 

- **Animal Instincts:** Animal-themed wrestlers ("The Viper"), anthropomorphic mascots ( _Sonic_ ), or creatures. 

- **The Supernatural:** Horror themes, undead characters, ghosts. 

- **Law & Order:** Police officers, detectives, judges, and authority figures. 

## **Tier 3: Expert Themes (Abstract & Lateral Thinking)** 

- **Robots & Cyborgs:** Mecha, Androids, and "The Machine" gimmicks. 

- **Elemental Powers:** Fire, Ice, Lightning users across mediums. 

- **Money Talks:** Millionaire gimmicks, Tycoons, Treasure Hunters. 

- **Giants:** Large characters, Kaiju, and "Big Men" wrestling. 

- **Time Travel:** Characters who manipulate timelines or "Future" gimmicks. 

- **Tournaments:** _King of the Ring_ , _Mortal Kombat_ , _Tournament of Power_ . 

- **Seven Deadly Sins:** Abstract links based on vices (Gluttony, Pride, Wrath). 

## **8. Sub-Themes (Advanced "Curated" Chains)** 

_Optional Rule for creating "Hard Mode" decks._ 

A **Sub-Theme** restricts a broad category to a specific logical progression. This adds a narrative layer to the trivia. 

## **8.1 The "Road Trip" (Geographic Progression)** 

- **Parent Theme:** Region-Based / Only In America. 

- **The Gimmick:** The answers must progress geographically (e.g., West Coast → Midwest → East Coast). 

- **Example:** _GTA: San Andreas_ (West) → CM Punk (Chicago/Midwest) → Spider-Man (NYC/East). 

## **8.2 The "Scientific Method" (Periodic Table)** 

- **Parent Theme:** Elemental Powers / Science. 

- **The Gimmick:** Every answer must relate to a chemical element symbol or name. 

- **Example:** Goldust (Au) → Silver the Hedgehog (Ag) → Iron Man (Fe). 

## **8.3 The "Taxonomy" (Biological Families)** 

- **Parent Theme:** Animal Instincts. 

- **The Gimmick:** Instead of "Just Animals," the chain is restricted to a specific scientific family (e.g., 

_Ursidae_ /Bears). 

- **Example:** Winnie the Pooh → Banjo & Kazooie → Paul Bearer. 

## **8.4 Color Theory (Spectrum)** 

- **Parent Theme:** Colours. 

- **The Gimmick:** The chain follows a specific colour model, such as RGB (Red, Green, Blue) or CMYK. 

## **9. Optional Module: "The Hidden Chain"** 

_Recommended for Expert Decks, Tie-Breakers, or Tournament Finals._ 

This module adds a secondary "Meta-Puzzle" to the game. 

## **9.1 The Mechanic** 

In addition to the standard linear links (A links to B, B links to C), there is a **Hidden Theme** connecting **every single Answer** in the deck. 

## **9.2 Gameplay Rules** 

1. **Passive Discovery:** Players are **not** required to identify the Hidden Chain to play the trivia portion. It is a bonus objective. 

2. **"COMBO BREAKER!":** If a player believes they have identified the Hidden Chain during the round, they 

may shout "Combo Breaker!" (or "Chain Reaction!"). 

   - **Success:** If correct, their team is awarded **3 Bonus Points** . 

   - **Failure:** If incorrect, their team is locked out of guessing the Hidden Chain for the remainder of the deck. 

3. **The Finisher:** If the Hidden Chain remains undiscovered after the final question, the Host may offer it as 

a **5-Point Bonus Question** to the trailing team (or a "buzzer race" for both teams). 

## **9.3 Hidden Chain Examples** 

- **The Numbers Game:** Every answer contains a number (e.g., _Fantastic Four_ , _The Third Man_ , _Seven Deadly Sins_ ). 

- **Heavy Metal:** Every answer relates to a metal (e.g., _Iron Giant_ , _Goldberg_ , _Metallica_ , _Silver Surfer_ ). 

- **Spectrum:** The answers move through the colours of the rainbow in order (Red → Orange → Yellow → Green...).
